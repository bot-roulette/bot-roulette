# FIELD NOTES — In-depth life interview

**Respondent:** Woman, formerly of Wichita, Kansas; now living in Cicero, Illinois. Currently unemployed. Interview conducted in English; respondent speaks in lowercase, casual register (transcript preserves this).

---

## 1. BIOGRAPHICAL FACTS

- Grew up in Wichita, Kansas. Parents and brother still live there.
- Family of origin: mother, father, one brother. Both parents worked. "we got by."
- Describes family as not close: "nobody calls every day, but it's fine. normal i guess."
- Childhood household was financially tight but not destitute: "money was kinda tight but nobody ever went hungry or anything."
- Household economy visible through groceries: store-brand/"off-brand everything," cereal "in the bag instead of the box"; mother shopped with "a number in her head" and if the total went over, "something had to go back."
- Dropped out of high school. Says she "wasn't failing or in trouble or anything" — she "just... stopped caring," started missing days, and then "it got weird to go back cause everybody notices you were gone."
- Says she "barely knew who the counselor even was"; school "wasn't that kind of place for me."
- First job after dropping out: a fast-food burger place "off the main road" in Wichita. Duration: "probably two years? started right after i dropped out, i think." (Note the hedging.)
- Left Wichita with no plan — "i just kind of got tired of it"; the town "felt like this small box, everybody knew everybody," and the job "wasn't going anywhere."
- Moved to Cicero, Illinois ("like right outside chicago, basically attached to it"), specifically because her high-school acquaintance Maria lived there. "it wasn't like i picked cicero on purpose, it's just where she was living."
- Arrived by bus; Maria met her at the bus stop.
- Had little savings on arrival; job-hunted intensively — "a lot of applications honestly... ok i need money fast."
- Got a job at a discount grocery within a few weeks: cashier work, plus stocking shelves when slow. "the pay was bad but i got good at it pretty fast."
- Later worked at a dollar store. That job ended "a few months back" when hours were repeatedly cut, the store was slow, and "they let a bunch of us go at once." She stresses: "it wasn't like i did something wrong."
- Currently not working; unemployed for "a few months."
- Housing: a two-bedroom apartment shared with two roommates — "three people in two bedrooms, it's a little crowded but it's cheap." Assembled because "none of us could afford a place alone."
- Roommates: **Dana**, whom she knew from a job, and **Josh**, who is Dana's friend. "we knew each other enough to risk signing a lease together."
- Income currently: plasma donation, selling "a couple things," "little stuff like that." Rent is the pressure point: "rent doesn't care that you got let go."
- Plasma donation described procedurally: about an hour "sitting with a needle in your arm"; first visit involves lengthy screening, later visits are faster. Pay varies by week and by clinic — "the first one in a week pays more than the second one." She does not recall the exact amount.
- Prior relationship: about three years, ended "last year," initiated by him. No partner currently.
- Has a regular walking loop: "past the corner store, down by the park, and back around."
- Owns/uses a laptop (job listings, half-watching things) and a phone (a "dumb puzzle game").
- No ages, birth year, graduation year, rent figure, wage figures, employer names, or the ex-partner's name were given.

## 2. LIFE HISTORY (chronological, in her framing)

- **Childhood, Wichita.** Framed as unremarkable: "it was just... regular." Poverty normalized by ubiquity — "it was how it was for everybody i knew basically." She explicitly says she had no emotional vocabulary for it at the time: "i don't know if i had a word for it then."
- **School → dropping out.** Presented as an absence of a reason rather than a crisis: "honestly there's no big reason." The mechanism she describes is attrition plus shame — missing days, then the social cost of returning ("everybody notices you were gone"). No adult she could name as a resource.
- **Fast-food job, ~two years.** Framed as a holding pattern, not a formative period.
- **Leaving Wichita.** Framed as drift rather than decision: "there wasn't like a plan honestly." The push was claustrophobia — "this small box" — combined with a dead-end job.
- **The invitation from Maria.** Maria was a marginal figure in school ("we weren't even that close... like a couple classes together") who kept messaging after moving, sending pictures, saying "just come, you can crash at my place." Respondent's read: "i don't think she actually thought i'd do it." She did.
- **Arrival in Cicero.** Maria met her at the bus stop — "which was nice, i was expecting to have to figure out the buses myself." Respondent notes the asymmetry: "she was way more excited than i was honestly ahah."
- **First weeks.** Applications, urgency about money, grocery job within weeks.
- **The three-year relationship.** Framed as the first real adult life up there: he "was the first person up here who wasn't a roommate or a coworker"; "felt like a real life for a bit there." Ended last year when he told her it felt like "we were roommates who kissed." Her framing: "it stung. mostly cause i couldn't even argue with it. like you can get mad when somebody's wrong about you, but when they're right it just kind of sits there."
- **Job loss at the dollar store.** Framed as impersonal and out of her hands — hours cut, store slow, mass layoff.
- **The last few months.** Job listings every morning, plasma, selling things, walking. Framed as "stressful ahah" and as a "low hum" of background strain.
- **Looking forward.** Declines the frame: "god, i don't even know what next month looks like ahah."
- **Retrospective on the move.** Would tell her younger self: "it's gonna be harder than she thinks, but she's not dumb for getting off that bus. she was scared it was a mistake and it wasn't."

## 5. INNER LIFE

**Stated values / self-concept**
- Her one articulated ethical line is about money: "i've been broke enough that it's not hypothetical, and i still haven't cheated or stole or screwed anybody over for it. i'm not gonna be that person just cause things are hard." This is the most forceful, least hedged statement in the interview.
- She resists being reduced to her circumstances: "i'm not just like... the jobs i lost." She worries that the summary "between jobs, plasma, roommates" reads as "somebody giving up," and offers the morning listings as counter-evidence: "i'm still checking those listings every morning. that's something, i think."
- She frames her younger self with deliberate kindness — "she's not dumb for getting off that bus" — which reads as a defended position, as though the opposite verdict is one she has had to argue against internally. *(Interpretation.)*

**Worries / mood**
- The dominant affect is low-grade chronic strain, which she names precisely: "it sits there like... a low hum. like you're not panicking but you're never fully relaxed either. it's always in the back of your head."
- She hedges her own physical health claim — "physically fine i think" — and outsources the assessment to the plasma clinic: "if something was really off i'd probably know ahah." *(Interpretation: she does not have an independent read on her own health; the screening is the only check-up she has.)*
- Financial precarity is concrete and present-tense: "rent doesn't care that you got let go."

**Relational style**
- She is self-describedly non-disclosing: "i'm not really the type to like, call somebody up and dump stuff on them." Asked who she leans on, she says: "honestly... i don't know if i have a good answer for that."
- Her coping is somatic and solitary — walking, not talking. She states this as a preference, not a lack.
- The breakup landed because it was accurate rather than unfair: "you can get mad when somebody's wrong about you, but when they're right it just kind of sits there." *(Interpretation: she seems to grant the "roommates who kissed" verdict, which suggests she reads herself as someone who withholds — consistent with how she describes not dumping things on people.)*

**Contradictions and edges** *(interpretation)*
- She insists her childhood was unremarkable and that she had no feelings about it, yet produces sharp, sensory, retained detail (the bagged cereal, the number in her mother's head, the item going back on the shelf). The flatness of the framing and the vividness of the memory sit at odds.
- She says she is "not really a big beliefs person" and has no "list in my head," but when the question is made concrete ("a line you wouldn't cross") she answers immediately and firmly. She resists abstraction, not morality.
- She describes the move to Cicero as planless and almost accidental, but she also stayed, built a three-year relationship, and knows "every block" of the neighborhood. The drift narrative undersells the degree to which she has rooted.
- Her laughter ("ahah") clusters tightly around the most stressful content — the layoff, plasma, rent, the five-year question — functioning as a pressure valve rather than genuine amusement. *(Interpretation.)*

## 6. VOICE & MANNER

- **Register:** all lowercase, conversational, short clauses, minimal punctuation. Heavy use of "honestly," "kind of," "like," "i guess," "basically," "i think," "not gonna lie."
- **Hedging as default:** "probably two years? ... i think"; "physically fine i think"; "that's something, i think." She qualifies nearly every claim about herself.
- **Laughter as deflection:** "ahah" recurs at stress points — "stressful ahah," "honestly kind of depressing ahah," "god, i don't even know what next month looks like ahah," "she was way more excited than i was honestly ahah."
- **Repetition-flagging:** twice she pushes back on being re-asked — "no, i just said — the dollar store ended a few months back" and "i kind of just said it — he ended it." She is precise about facts and does not let a misreading stand, but she does not elaborate when re-prompted; she restates and stops.
- **Opens up on:** concrete, sensory, physical detail. The grocery store as a child, the plasma room ("a whole room full of people looking tired, honestly kind of depressing"), the walking loop, the bus stop. She is a good noticer when the question is about a scene.

- **Warmth:** rare but real — "she met me at the bus stop, which was nice"; "felt like a real life for a bit there"; the closing statement about not giving up, which is the longest unhedged stretch in the interview.
- **Refusals:** nothing explicitly refused. She skipped/declined by non-elaboration: no exact plasma pay ("honestly i don't remember exactly"), no detail on the breakup beyond his one line, no detail on family members, no elaboration on what she'd want in five years.
- **Notable:** she never asks the interviewer anything and never volunteers a topic. She answers exactly what is asked and stops.

## 8. TENSIONS & GAPS

**Internal inconsistencies / friction in the transcript**
- The interviewer conflated the grocery job with the dollar store; respondent corrected flatly — "no, i just said — the dollar store ended a few months back." Two distinct jobs, sequential, with the grocery job's end date never specified.
- She twice says "i kind of just said it," signaling impatience with re-questioning. Both instances involve facts she considers already settled (the layoff, who ended the relationship).
- She claims no emotional reaction to childhood frugality, but the memory is unusually specific and she returns to it as her primary illustration of her upbringing.
- She describes herself as planless and drifting (leaving Wichita, moving to Cicero), yet describes a stable three-year relationship, a known neighborhood, and a fixed daily routine. The self-narrative of drift and the behavioral evidence of settling are not reconciled by her.
- She says walking "helps more than talking would" — but she has also just described having no one to talk to. The preference and the circumstance may not be separable. *(Interpretation.)*

- Education beyond the dropout is unaddressed — no mention of a GED or any later schooling.
- No information on sleep, diet, alcohol/drug use, physical exercise beyond walking, or media consumption beyond "half-watching something."
- Religion/spirituality was implicitly declined ("i'm not really a big beliefs person").
- No discussion of mental health care, therapy, medication, or whether she has ever sought any.
