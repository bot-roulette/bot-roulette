# FIELD NOTES — “Mike”

## 1. BIOGRAPHICAL FACTS

- Name used by interviewer at close: **Mike**. No surname given.
- Age: **41** (stated twice, unprompted).
- Grew up **about eight miles outside a town of roughly 550 people in northeast Pennsylvania** — “dirt road, firewood, school bus.”
- Household growing up: **four people** — respondent, one older sibling, mother, father.
- Father: drove the **school bus** and **sold firewood**. Now “basically retired from the bus” but “still cuts firewood because he can't sit still.”
- Mother: worked at **a bank**; now “out of the bank.”
- Parents **still live in the county**.
- Respondent was **the younger child**; got the “easy jobs at first, stacking and not the splitting” of firewood.
- Post-secondary path: **Penn College for a year** (“kind of spun my wheels”), **took a semester off**, then **Mansfield** for the actual degree in **graphic design**.
- Describes Williamsport as “just a bigger town, not exactly the big city.”
- Moved to **Philadelphia** after college; worked **a couple of short contracts at small agencies** (“a few months here, a few months there”) plus **retail evenings** to make rent.
- Lived in **Bristol, England** for **“maybe a year and a half”** on a small-studio design gig; also **pulled bar shifts** there.
- **Returned in 2014**; says the return “was supposed to be temporary” and he “just sort of... stayed.”
- Was **32 when he came back** (his words: “i came back at 32, broke, single”).
- A **seven-year relationship ended in 2015** — “no big blowup, nobody cheated.”
- Met current partner around **2016** (“i was still kind of a mess in 2016”).
- Current partner works at a **vet clinic in Towanda**; has “the real job with the clinic, benefits and all.”
- **Two children, ages nine and six.** The six-year-old is referred to as “she.”
- Lives in a **rented farmhouse outside town** — “old place, drafty in spots”; landlord described as “decent.”
- Has **thought about buying** but says “the math on a patchwork income for a mortgage... the bank looks at you like you're joking.”
- Income streams named: **freelance design on the laptop**, **hand-painted sign work and installations**, **teaching one class a semester at the community college** (fall and spring), **layout for a shopper newspaper** (“steady-ish”), and **marketing work for a camp**.
- Sequence he gives for how the patchwork accreted: “sign work turned into the shopper newspaper layout turned into the camp marketing turned into teaching.”
- Plays **bass in a cover band**; gigs at **fire halls and county fairs**. Started in his **thirties**, **no lessons**.
- Owns a **truck** that needs brakes.
- Keeps a **spreadsheet**, invoices everything, and **sets aside money for taxes** with each check; pays **quarterly taxes**.
- Hobbies named: **gardening** (summer), **trout fishing** (spring), **band** (year-round).
- No dollar figures of any kind were given — no income, no rent, no gig pay, no adjunct rate.

## 2. LIFE HISTORY

- **Childhood (0–18), dirt road outside a 550-person town.** Framed as “quiet, honestly,” with the double edge of everybody knowing everybody: “you couldn't get away with much, but if your truck broke down someone would just show up with a chain without even asking. i miss that part.” Mostly remembers “being outside a lot” and helping his father with firewood, which he calls “kind of the family thing.”
- **College.** Chose art “mostly because i was good at drawing and didn't know what else to do with it,” and graphic design because it “sounded more practical than it turned out to be.” Framed the whole choice retrospectively as a self-deception: “‘practical’ was kind of a story i told my parents and myself.”
- **Philadelphia.** The move is framed as the genuine threshold — “the real jump was later... that one actually scared me.” Motivation: “i wanted to see if i could actually do this design thing somewhere that mattered. towanda sign shops weren't gonna tell me that.” Landing was “rough at first honestly. like loud and fast and nobody waiting on you.” Finding people was “slow, honestly. i'm not a natural joiner.”
- **Bristol (~1.5 years).** Framed as small and opportunistic, not a career move: “it wasn't a big career move, it was more like I knew someone who knew someone.” Same economic life as Philadelphia, “but with a different accent around me.”
- **Return, 2014.** Framed as **both homecoming and retreat**: “both, if i'm honest... some of it was retreating. the money wasn't working, i was tired, and i came back to the safe thing. i don't think i admitted that to myself for a couple years.”
- **2015 — the breakup.** “It just slowly stopped working and then one day we both knew it was done.” He notes the absence of a villain makes it harder: “there's nothing to be angry at. it just didn't hold.” Followed by a low stretch: “i was pretty low for a while after. didn't do much of anything for a stretch there.”
- **2016 — meeting his partner.** Framed as a slow, unlikely fit: “she looked like she'd rather be anywhere else and then she stayed till the last song.” He was “still kind of a mess.” “Took me way too long to actually ask her out though.”
- **Building it back.** The central redemptive arc, stated as his proudest achievement: “i built a life back after a bad couple of years, and didn't just... quit on the whole thing. nobody handed me the life i've got now, i pieced it together after coming home broke and single.”
- **Present.** Patchwork work, two kids, band, garden, rented farmhouse, and a low background anxiety about retirement and his knees.

## 4. DAILY LIFE & HABITS

- **Wake ~6:30**, driven by the kids. Getting them out the door to school is “the fixed point” of the day.
- Work week is variable by design: laptop freelance, on-site sign painting/installation, and **one day a week teaching in fall and spring**. Partner's clinic schedule is “more real than mine.”
- Evenings: dinner, kids, **garden in summer**; many nights **at a fire hall with the band** or **in the basement with the bass** when there's no gig.
- **Sleep is poor.** “I'm up thinking about the invoices or the truck or whatever at 2am sometimes.”
- **Coping routine for 2am anxiety:** get up and **make a list** — “try to turn the hum into something concrete i can look at.” Says it “sometimes... helps, sometimes it's just anxiety with better handwriting.” What actually works: “fixing one small thing. send the invoice, make the call i've been dreading.” Also: “a beer sometimes, which i know isn't the answer. mostly the list though.”
- **Money management:** spreadsheet, invoices everything, sets aside tax money from every check, “been doing it long enough that it's just habit now.” Bad months are **winter**, when sign work dries up. Strategy: “you learn to make a fat month cover two thin ones.”
- **Deferral economy in thin months:** “nothing gets ‘cut’ exactly, it's more deferring. the truck needs brakes, well, it can wait another month. the furnace, the dentist, new boots for the kid, all of it slides to the right.”
- **Food:** “the garden helps in summer, we eat a lot out of that.”
- **Seasonal rhythm:** garden (summer), trout fishing (spring), band (year round).
- **Consumption:** beer mentioned once, framed as a not-great habit. No other drinking, smoking, or media habits discussed.
- **No church.** “Not a church guy.”

## 5. INNER LIFE

**Core self-concept: fragmentation.**
- The most revealing formulation in the interview: “designer, sign painter, teacher, band guy, dad. that's five. and none of them is a full-time thing... so i'm always kind of half in each one.”
- Consequence: “some days that's fine and it keeps things interesting. other days it feels like i'm not really from anywhere.”
- The band is the exception: “the closest i get to feeling like i'm from one thing instead of five,” because “nobody there cares what else i do.”
- *Interpretation:* the “five” line was produced spontaneously (“ha, i don't know, it just kind of came out”) and then immediately elaborated with precision — this is a formulation he has half-thought before but perhaps never said aloud. It reads as the interview's organizing insight.

**Values (stated explicitly).**
- “Do it right even when nobody's checking.” He grounds this in craft: “the prep behind a sign, nobody ever sees it, but the sign's either gonna hold for ten years or it isn't based on that and nothing else.”
- “Show up when you said you would. that one's simple and most people don't do it.” He names this as the main thing he'd want the kids to absorb.
- He explicitly declines a tidy moral framework: “not a church guy, so i don't have a tidy answer for this one.”

**Money as ambient dread.**
- “The money stuff sits in the back of my head like a hum you stop noticing until it's real loud.”
- His own summary of financial life: “it's more like never quite catching your breath than actually falling.” He is careful to soften it — “i don't want to make it sound grim.”
- The future-worry is specifically about **aging in a patchwork**: “i'm 41 and there's no retirement worth the name... patchwork at 46 is the same patchwork with older knees and the same truck that needs brakes.” He adds: “i try not to stare at it too long.”

**Agency and its cost.**
- On whether the patchwork was chosen: “piece by piece, mostly. i'd love to say i designed it, but no.” Then the reconciliation: “i chose the freedom and the freedom came with the rest of it. can't really have one without the other.”
- *Interpretation:* he holds two positions at once — that the patchwork happened to him and that he chose it — and resolves the contradiction by reframing it as a package deal rather than pretending one is true.

**Regret, carefully bounded.**
- On the design degree: “i don't regret it. i'd probably have been miserable as an accountant. but there's a version where i learned a trade and had benefits and my knees would thank me. i think about that sometimes, especially in january.”
- *Interpretation:* “especially in january” recurs as his shorthand for the annual low point (cf. “ask me in january when the quarterly taxes are due”). It functions as a private calendar of dread.

**Belonging and claustrophobia.**
- Being back in the county is “kind of grounding and kind of claustrophobic depending on the day.”
- The claustrophobia is specifically about **reputation freeze**: “i came back at 32, broke, single, living in my parents' county again. and in a town this size, people kind of freeze you at that. like i'm always going to be the guy who went away and came back. nobody says it, but you feel it.”
- The wish underneath it: “some days you just want to walk into a store and be a stranger for once.”

**Pride.**
- First answer is recovery, not achievement: “that i built a life back after a bad couple of years, and didn't just... quit on the whole thing.”
- Second is the bass, and he insists on sole ownership of it: “i picked up an instrument in my thirties with no lessons and now i can play a whole night at a fire hall and people dance. that one's mine entirely.”
- Kids are named third and immediately de-centered: “i can't take all the credit there.”

**Self-deprecation as a stabilizer.**
- “I'm not gonna pretend i'm some musician. i'm a guy who knows where the root note is and stays out of the way, which is honestly 80% of playing bass in a cover band.”
- “Anxiety with better handwriting.” “Fun times.” These are the jokes he reaches for when the material gets close.

**Politics / the wider world — deliberately shallow.**
- “I don't think i fit neatly into either version of the country people are always arguing about on tv. i'm rural but i'm not, i don't know, i don't check all the boxes people assume when they hear northeast PA.”
- “I mostly think about it small. my town, my kids' school, whether the people around me are doing okay. the big picture stuff, i've kind of stopped trusting anyone who's real sure about it.”
- *Interpretation:* this is a genuine epistemic stance, not just evasion — but it is also the one place he actively capped the conversation.

**The closing self-characterization — the thesis he wanted on the record.**
- “It's not a sad story. i know it sounds like a lot of hum and thin months and juggling, but it's not a sad story. it's a messy one.”
- “Nobody hands you the tidy version anyway. the messy one was the real one the whole time.”

## 6. VOICE & MANNER

- **Register:** writes/speaks in lowercase, conversational, unpunctuated clauses. Opens with a joke about the format: “huh, the whole thing? that's a lot to fit in a text message.”
- **Verbal tics:** “honestly” is constant — often as a sincerity marker before a confession (“honestly the first school wasn't much of a leap”; “honestly? it's a juggling act”; “honestly? that i built a life back”). Also “if i'm being straight with you,” “if i'm honest,” “i don't know,” “kind of,” “sort of,” “mostly.”
- **Hedging as architecture:** he softens nearly every claim with a qualifier, then lands a hard, well-made sentence. Pattern: hedge → concrete image. (“it's less dramatic than it sounds... it's more like never quite catching your breath than actually falling.”)
- **Humor:** dry, self-directed, never at others' expense. “Anxiety with better handwriting.” “Fun times.” “Which honestly shows she's got a future in something.” “The bank looks at you like you're joking.”
- **Metaphor:** consistently physical and mechanical — hum, juggling, fat month/thin month, catching your breath, a chain showing up, “loud enough to drown all that out,” “landing on the ground, which is different.”
- **Corrects the interviewer's framing** rather than accepting it: when told the degree seemed practical — “funny you say practical, because it wasn't really.” When offered “coming home or retreating” — “both, if i'm honest.” He resists being handed a tidy version.
- **Opens up on:** the band, the kids, the garden, the craft of sign work, the mechanics of money, the town's changes, the “five” formulation. Physical, concrete, hands-based topics unlock him.
- **Shuts down / fences off:**
  - The ex-relationship, first pass: “that's kind of its own story and not a happy-ending one, so, i don't know. maybe we save it for later if it comes up.” Second pass, after being invited: gives the facts, then closes the door — “that's about as much as i've got in me on it right now, if i'm being straight with you.”
  - Politics: “that's as far as i want to wade in, if that's alright.”
  - Religion: one line, no elaboration.
- **Notable reaction:** the health/wellbeing question visibly surprises him — “that's a question nobody asks me, huh” — and then he answers it more candidly than anything else in the interview.
- **Deflects praise.** When told the “five” line was striking: “ha, i don't know, it just kind of came out.” When credited with the kids: “half of that's her being the steady one.”
- **Ends on his own terms.** He does not let the interviewer's sympathetic framing stand; he overrides it with “it's not a sad story.”

## 8. TENSIONS & GAPS

**Internal inconsistencies / soft contradictions**
- **Chosen vs. happened:** “piece by piece, mostly. i'd love to say i designed it, but no” sits against “i chose the freedom and the freedom came with the rest of it.” He resolves it rhetorically rather than logically.
- **Homecoming vs. retreat:** he names both, then admits he suppressed the retreat reading — “i don't think i admitted that to myself for a couple years.”
- **How he met his partner:** “a friend's cookout, kind of, but the real meet was she came out to a show.” Two origin stories offered in one breath, the first immediately downgraded.
- **The degree:** “it wasn't really practical” vs. “the degree got me in the door at sign shops and it taught me to see, which matters.” Both are held as true.
- **“Not a sad story”** is asserted against a transcript that is substantially about exhaustion, deferral, and 2am dread. He knows this and pre-empts it — which suggests the framing is partly defensive, partly earned. *(Interpretation.)*

**Deliberate omissions**
- **The ex-relationship** is the clearest walled-off area. No name, no gender, no occupation, no account of how it began, no account of the low period beyond “didn't do much of anything for a stretch.” He explicitly limits it: “that's about as much as i've got in me on it right now.”
- **Politics and the wider world** — he engages just far enough to state a stance and then stops: “that's as far as i want to wade in.”
- **Religion/faith** — one line (“not a church guy”), no exploration.
- **His mother** — mentioned twice, functionally, never characterized. His father gets texture (“can't sit still”); his mother gets a job title.
- **The older sibling** — “drifted the way you do” is the whole account. No name, no gender, no current contact, no incident.

**Vagueness / unresolved threads**
- **Bristol** is oddly thin for a year and a half abroad: “sort of” a studio gig, “someone who knew someone,” no name, no city detail beyond “Bristol,” no relationships formed there.
- **The Philadelphia years** are compressed and undated. It is unclear when he moved there, how long he stayed, whether he went to Bristol from Philadelphia, or how the seven-year relationship maps onto Philadelphia/Bristol/the return. The relationship ran roughly 2008–2015 by arithmetic, which would span the Bristol period and the return — but he never says so.
- **The shopper newspaper** and **the camp marketing** are named once each as income sources and never explained — what they are, how he got them, whether they continue.
- **The band** is emotionally central but logistically blank: no band name, no gig frequency, no pay, no bandmate names, no repertoire beyond “songs that were old when we learned them.”
- **No dollar amounts anywhere.** No income, rent, mortgage target, adjunct rate, gig pay, or tax burden. He talks fluently about the *mechanics* of money and never about *figures*.
- **Health** is only “tired more than i used to be” and poor sleep. No conditions, no doctor, no exercise beyond gardening and fishing.
- **Friends** are essentially absent as named individuals — “some people through work,” “roommates of roommates,” “a buddy.” For a man who describes his town as a place where everyone knows everyone, no friend is ever named.
- **Ages/dates of parents**, the sibling's life, and whether the kids are his biological children (never stated either way) are all untouched.
- **The “five”** — he disclaims it (“i don't know, it just kind of came out”) and then delivers it as a fully formed list. Worth probing if there's a follow-up: whether the five feel ranked, and which one he'd keep.

**Topics never covered**
- Any conflict with his parents or in childhood; the childhood is rendered almost entirely as texture (dirt road, firewood, outside).
- Sibling's current life or the cause of the drift.
- The end of the Philadelphia period, or why he left for Bristol rather than staying.
- Whether he and his partner are married (he says “partner” throughout; no wedding mentioned).
- Childcare logistics, school district, or the kids' school experiences.
- Any experience of the 2016–present period beyond the bare outline — the entire last decade is summarized as “built a life back” with almost no events.
- Whether he still does design work he cares about, versus design as one more check.
- What he'd do with money if the hum stopped.
